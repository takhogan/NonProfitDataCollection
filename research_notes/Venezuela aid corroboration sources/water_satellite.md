# Open satellite water and rainfall evidence

## Which freely available sources are operationally useful in Venezuela?

### Takeaway
Use rainfall as catchment context and optical/radar imagery as evidence about visible surface water, not piped-water service. The first pilot should prioritize CHIRPS v3 for seasonal context, optionally IMERG for recent rain, and a narrowly selected reservoir time series only after the NGO confirms its supply connection. Research checked 7 November 2026; no accounts, paid processing, or integrations created.

### Cited Findings
- **Sentinel-2 optical:** 10 m bands (visible/NIR), 20 m bands including SWIR, and 60 m atmospheric bands; nominal two-satellite revisit five days at equator. Covers global land including Venezuela; public/general/scientific/commercial users receive data free. Current mission page describes S2B/S2C and temporary S2A operation. A 10 m rendered image does not make SWIR water analysis 10 m native resolution. [CDSE Sentinel-2](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/data-collections/copernicus-sentinel-missions/sentinel-2)
- L2A global operational coverage began 13 December 2018; ESA states its historical 2015–December 2023 archive has now been reprocessed into Collection-1 L1C and L2A. The CDSE collection landing table still labels original L2A “Europe”; use the detailed ESA product documentation and actual catalog, not that table alone, when verifying a Venezuelan AOI. [ESA S2 products](https://sentiwiki.copernicus.eu/web/s2-products)
- **Sentinel-1 radar:** all-weather/day-night acquisition; archive begins with 2014 mission. Single-satellite orbit repeat 12 days, two-satellite exact repeat six days; ESA explicitly distinguishes potential global coverage from operational acquisition baseline. Do not promise a six-day Venezuelan observation without catalog inspection. [ESA S1 mission](https://sentiwiki.copernicus.eu/web/s1-mission) [ASF monthly acquisition maps](https://asf.alaska.edu/daac/sentinel-1-acquisition-maps/)
- Sentinel-1 IW GRD high-resolution product has roughly **20 × 22 m spatial resolution and 10 × 10 m pixel spacing**. Do not call its 10 m grid a 10 m resolving capability. [ESA S1 products](https://sentiwiki.copernicus.eu/web/s1-products)
- CDSE expects Sentinel-1/2 availability within 24 hours of sensing and links live timeliness metrics. This is delivery latency *after* a successful acquisition, not a new observation every day or an SLA. [CDSE FAQ](https://documentation.dataspace.copernicus.eu/FAQ.html)
- Sentinel licenses allow reproduction, redistribution, modification and combinations, subject to lawful use and limited exceptions, without suitability warranty. Retain prescribed Copernicus attribution, including modified-data wording where applicable. [Sentinel legal notice](https://cds.climate.copernicus.eu/licences/ec-sentinel)
- CDSE offers STAC/OData/S3 access plus processing APIs; general-user limits currently include 12 TB rolling transfer, four download connections, 10,000 Sentinel Hub requests and processing units monthly, and a **temporary** 10,000 openEO monthly credits. Free processing is quota-bound, not unlimited; batch API availability differs. Offline archive items require Data Workspace ordering. [CDSE quotas](https://documentation.dataspace.copernicus.eu/Quotas.html)
- **Landsat:** worldwide archive; 30 m multispectral, nominal 16-day repeat per satellite. Useful for long baselines, not household pipes. [USGS mission overview](https://eros.usgs.gov/earthshots/the-landsat-program) [USGS data access](https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-data-access)
- Current USGS Level-2 processing guidance: availability typically 24–72 h after Tier 1/2; Landsat 9 Tier 1/2 in 4–6 h and L2 about three days; Landsat 8 Tier 1/2 approximately 14–16 days while refined TIRS parameters are applied. Do not copy old generic “hours” claims into L2 operational promises. [USGS L2 products](https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-collection-2-level-2-science-products)
- Landsat downloads are no-cost, including EarthExplorer; machine access and cloud paths are documented. Imagery use/redistribution is unrestricted with USGS credit requested. Hosting, egress, storage and analyst effort remain separate costs. [USGS access](https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-data-access) [USGS cloud guide, section 6](https://d9-wret.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/assets/palladium/production/s3fs-public/atoms/files/LSDS-2032-Landsat-Commercial-Cloud-Direct-Access-Users-Guide-v2.pdf.pdf)
- **JRC Global Surface Water:** release page updated 15 October 2026 now covers 1984–2024, 30 m Landsat-derived history; this is a historical baseline, not current outage monitoring. GeoTIFF downloads, scripts, Earth Engine and WMTS available; WMTS colors are unsuitable for numerical analysis. Open/no charge with EC JRC/Google and scholarly attribution. V1.5 changes include Collection 1/2 spatial offsets, potentially at least a pixel; monthly/yearly history for 2022–24 must be joined with older assets; monthly recurrence has a known sparse-observation issue. [JRC access and release notes](https://global-surface-water.appspot.com/download)
- **CHIRPS v3:** 0.05° land rainfall grid (~5.5 km north–south), 60°N–60°S/all longitudes, 1981–near present, hence Venezuela covered. Preliminary released two days after a pentad ends; final typically third week of following month. GeoTIFF/COG/NetCDF/BIL and direct repository downloads. Fundamentally pentad/monthly; daily data are disaggregated. Daily “sat” uses IMERG Late V07 and “rnl” uses ERA5; daily date labels are not uniform local gauge observation windows. Provider uses both public-domain and CC BY 4.0 wording: preserve attribution and license text conservatively. [CHC v3](https://www.chc.ucsb.edu/data/chirps3)
- CHIRPS v2 production is scheduled to end after December 2026; build a new pipeline on v3 and avoid splicing versions without calibration. Satellite rainfall has terrain bias and can underestimate extreme precipitation. [CHC v2 transition and caveats](https://chc.ucsb.edu/data/chirps)
- **IMERG V07B:** global including Venezuela, 0.1° (~11 km), half-hour estimates; reprocessed record to January 1998. Check selected archive/product because older GIS/daily landing pages still say June 2000. [NASA current Early Run](https://gpm.nasa.gov/taxonomy/term/1357) [GIS daily product](https://pps.gsfc.nasa.gov/Documents/V07/doi/3IMERGHH_EARLY_GIS_1DAY_V07.html)
- NASA nominal latency Early ~4 h, Late ~14 h (latency FAQ notes ~12 h in then-recent data), Final ~3.5 months. These are differing estimates/revisions of the same event, not independent confirmations. [NASA latency explanation](https://gpm.nasa.gov/resources/faq/what-determines-latency-imerg) [NASA September 2026 support answer](https://forum.earthdata.nasa.gov/viewtopic.php?sid=9122562048ee71d4bfaadf80ac131429&t=8183)
- GPM data are freely available, source/version attribution expected; NASA asks secondary-archive users to verify current, accurate copies. GES DISC provides online archive, Earthdata Search, Giovanni, OPeNDAP and subsetting; data access can require free Earthdata login. This is general public access, not a nonprofit grant. [GPM policy](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/policy) [GES DISC daily product](https://disc.gsfc.nasa.gov/datasets/GPM_3IMERGDF_07/summary?keywords=imerg) [NASA IMERG access](https://gpm.nasa.gov/data/imerg)

### Inferences
- Within the overall shortlist, rainfall/context ranks below local operational evidence. CHIRPS is easiest to process at low bandwidth; use pentad/monthly anomalies on known catchments. Add IMERG only if recent storm/flood context changes coordination decisions.
- Sentinel-2 reservoir extent is conditional value: local supply-zone/catchment linkage must be known. Sentinel-1 is useful where clouds prevent optical observation, but interpretation/terrain correction cost is higher. Landsat/JRC mainly improve historical baseline. A nearby lake is not necessarily the source of a community's tap supply.
- $0 imagery does not equal $0 service: budget analyst review, download bandwidth, processing, local disk, monitored jobs and backups. For a tiny AOI, local processing avoids a paid cloud dependency. No verified per-AOI runtime or cost benchmark was performed; give a budget cap rather than invented recurring dollar estimates.

### Gaps
- No pilot communities/reservoirs specified, so actual Sentinel acquisition count, cloud-free opportunities, catalog lag distribution, and service-zone alignment cannot yet be measured. Country-level footprint availability is not parcel-level usable coverage.
- No live API authentication/download experiment performed, no uptime/SLA established, and no current Venezuela rain-gauge density audit. These remain explicit POC tests.

## What can these sources actually corroborate?

### Takeaway
They can support claims about rainfall, flood extent and large exposed surface-water change. They cannot verify household electricity, tap pressure/availability, potable quality, delivered water, or individual aid need. No signal or conflicting context must never trigger automatic denial.

### Cited Findings
- Optical flood mapping is cloud-limited; radar penetrates cloud but buildings, smooth surfaces and radar shadow may resemble water, and dense vegetation obstructs observations. [ESA water mapping limitations](https://eo4society.esa.int/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/EOC0020_WOR_v1.pdf) [UN-SPIDER SAR practice](https://un-spider.org/advisory-support/recommended-practices/recommended-practice-flood-mapping/in-detail)
- NASA notes threshold-based SAR water mapping limitations, including surface conditions and vegetation; this requires analyst interpretation rather than equating dark pixels with a reliable supply. [NASA SAR training](https://appliedsciences.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/2023-11/SAR_Part3.pdf)

### Inferences
- A reservoir-area reduction is evidence of reduced visible extent only. Inferring storage volume requires validated bathymetry/elevation/level relationships; inferring tap supply additionally requires intake/pump/power/treatment/distribution operation. Full reservoirs can coexist with dry taps; rainfall can coexist with failed pumps or unsafe drinking water.
- Do not label a reservoir anomaly as “supports household piped-water outage” by default. Use `context_for` at catchment/supply-system level, with explicit linkage quality. Only direct utility/field evidence can more closely corroborate service availability.
- Compare same-season histories, comparable sensor geometry and products, valid-pixel fractions, and uncertainty intervals. Distinguish unobserved from observed no change. Cloud masks should apply to the reservoir AOI, not merely scene-wide cloud percentage.
- Neighboring administrative regions need not share the same supply network. Area-weight rainfall over the actual catchment; do not assign a nearest coarse pixel to a household as household evidence.
- CHIRPS daily “sat” and IMERG share upstream data. Record lineage to avoid counting them as independent votes. JRC and Landsat likewise share imagery; multiple hosting catalogs do not create independence.

### Gaps
- No defensible inference of water safety from these datasets. In-situ testing/release procedures and recipient information remain necessary operational inputs.

## How should the platform pilot and validate this safely?

### Takeaway
Add small, inspectable context artifacts to the existing worker/review architecture after the intake and fulfillment slice. Validate usable coverage and decision utility before building continuous remote-sensing infrastructure.

### Cited Findings
- Existing project design already separates observations, evidence assessment, needs and assistance decisions, and assigns comparison jobs to the worker; domain logic is shared and organization scope enforced. [Platform plan](../../PLATFORM_PLAN.md) [Component architecture](../../components/README.md)

### Inferences
- Proposed bounded experiment: two communities plus one confirmed supplying catchment/reservoir; 6–8 weeks shadow operation and 12–24 months retrospective seasonal comparison. Start with public downloaded CHIRPS v3 pentads and one manually reviewed Sentinel-2 time series. Add radar only if measured cloud loss warrants it. This is a proposed design, not a completed analysis.
- Extend a source registry with provider/product/version/license/attribution and upstream lineage. Store external asset ID, acquisition start/end, product publication time, ingestion time, coverage geometry, native resolution versus output grid, mask/valid fraction, processing/version, source URL/checksum, provisional/final revision and artifact reference. Link via explicit evidence relations to area/episode/review, not by fabricating a household assessment.
- Preserve underlying values and units: rainfall mm over stated interval, visible water area m², anomaly baseline window and method, valid observations count, uncertain/unavailable reason. Keep raw satellite files out of the transactional relational database; store references and small summaries.
- Worker: idempotent ingest keyed source/product/asset/version; query only approved AOIs, retry with backoff and quota handling, track freshness and failures, retain past versions when preliminary becomes final. Domain: compatibility rules prevent service-level contradiction flags from mere rainfall/surface-water context. Web: evidence time, coverage, lineage and confidence reasons adjacent to map.
- POC validation targets (proposed, not empirically justified thresholds): 100% artifacts expose provenance/version/time/license; 100% unavailable/invalid coverage becomes unknown rather than zero; zero automated denial or need closure based on environmental evidence. Measure p50/p90 acquisition-to-publication, publication-to-ingestion, usable-coverage gap, reviewer time and cost per AOI.
- Separately validate water-mask output against human-labeled held-out imagery/field-visible shorelines where safe; report precision/recall and boundary uncertainty, stratified by clouds, vegetation and terrain. Do not score the mask against household tap availability: they are different targets.
- For operational value, have reviewers log whether context changed a follow-up or dispatch-planning action. Compare to local-report-only review on held-out episodes without exposing household identities; retain disagreement and absent-signal cases. A small pilot cannot establish general household-outage detection sensitivity, and should not claim it.

### Gaps
- Quantitative usable-observation and workload acceptance thresholds should be agreed after baseline measurement, not invented as source guarantees. No supply-network map or local authoritative reservoir-level series was verified in this task.
